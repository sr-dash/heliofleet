    Solar coverage

    Stonyhurst heliographic (HGS) and Carrington longitudes as seen from Earth. Footpoint is where a nominal Parker spiral at the chosen wind speed meets the Sun.

    Body r au HGS lon HGS lat Carr. lon ∠ Earth to Earth au Footpoint

    Enter an active region, flare site or filament. The table shows how far it sits from disk centre for each observer at the current moment and updates as you move in time. The angle is measured from the local vertical, so 0° is disk centre and 90° is the limb. Loops and CMEs well above the surface stay visible a little beyond 90°.

    Bodies

    Each row gives the heliocentric ecliptic J2000 position, Stonyhurst and Carrington coordinates, separation from Earth and the Parker-spiral footpoint at the current wind speed. Column definitions are written at the top of the file.

    Ephemerides from JPL Horizons, sampled daily (Parker every 3 h) and interpolated; predictions beyond each mission's tracking data are planning trajectories. Heliographic coordinates follow the IAU solar rotation model, as in SunPy.

    Flares, CMEs and geomagnetic storms, including the CME speeds, directions and widths used for the fronts on the map, come from the Space Weather Database Of Notifications, Knowledge, Information (DONKI), developed and maintained by the Community Coordinated Modeling Center at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center.
