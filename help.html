Getting started

HelioFleet has two working pages. The map shows where the spacecraft are around the Sun and Earth, what each of them can see of the solar surface, and research tools for coordinates, geometry and searches. The sky view shows instrument fields of view on the plane of the sky, and observation footprints of high-resolution instruments over SDO images. Both work for any moment since 2010.

  • Choose a moment with the date box, the step buttons (±1 hour, ±1 day), the timeline under the map, or a preset (eclipses, mission milestones, space-weather events). On the map, the arrow keys step one day, and with Shift one hour.
  • Today returns to the present; the HelioFleet title starts over with the default view.
  • The interface is in English, Hindi and Spanish, and has four colour themes; both are remembered.
  • Everything you change is written into the page address, so the address always reproduces what you see (see Sharing a view).

The map

Spacecraft and planets
Click a spacecraft or its name in the legend for its card: distance, heliographic coordinates, light time, the Sun's apparent size, perihelia, and buttons for its view of the Sun and its alignments. Legend buttons hide or show bodies.
Display
Earth-fixed or inertial frame, top or edge-on view, trails, planets, Parker spirals at a chosen solar-wind speed, CME fronts, and coronagraph and heliospheric-imager fields of view.
Solar coverage
Under the map, a Carrington map of the solar surface over the SDO/HMI synoptic magnetogram, with each observer's near and far side and the spacecraft footpoints. Hover for what each point is seen by; click to mark a point.
Snapshots, animations and embedding
PNG and PDF snapshots with a key, MP4 and GIF animations of a date range, a link to the current view, and code to embed the map on another site.

Research tools

The tabs under the map share the map's geometry, so every number matches what is drawn. Each tab can be opened directly with tab= in the address.

Coordinates (tab=coords)
Distance, Stonyhurst and Carrington coordinates, separation from Earth and Parker-spiral footpoint of every body, as CSV or JSON, with SunPy code that reproduces them.
Viewing geometry (tab=geom)
For co-aligning and time-aligning images from different viewpoints: each observer's light-time offset from Earth, apparent solar radius, B0, Stonyhurst longitude and L0 (as RSUN_OBS, HGLT_OBS, HGLN_OBS and CRLN_OBS in its FITS headers), the size of one arcsecond at disk centre, and how much larger features look than from Earth.
Find alignments (tab=align)
Searches a date range for radial alignments of two spacecraft, quadratures with Earth, or magnetic connections. The search is kept in the address (align=), so a shared link reruns it.
Who sees this spot (tab=vis)
For a Carrington or Stonyhurst location, which observers see it, how close to disk centre, and whether it is near the limb or behind it.
Export data (tab=series) and Animation (tab=anim)
Time series of positions and coordinates, and video of a date range.

Sky view

Plane of the sky
Instrument fields of view projected onto the plane of the sky seen from Earth or from a chosen spacecraft ("View from"), with MLSO K-Cor and UCoMP fields and Cryo-NIRSP footprints. Zoom with the buttons or the mouse wheel, drag to pan, double-click to centre. Layers turn each field on or off.
Who sees this point
Click the plane of the sky to mark a point; the panel lists every instrument whose field contains it, with distance from Sun centre and position angle in that instrument's own view.
Ground observatories and Cryo-NIRSP
Daylight windows at Haleakalā and Mauna Loa, and the DKIST Cryo-NIRSP datasets near the moment, with buttons to step to earlier or later observations.
Snapshot
PNG or PDF of the plane of the sky with a key of every layer, and a link to the current view.

Observations on SDO images

Below the plane of the sky, the fields of DKIST (Cryo-NIRSP, VBI, ViSP, DL-NIRSP, VTF), IRIS, Hinode/EIS and Solar Orbiter (EUI-HRI, PHI-HRT, SPICE) observations within 12 hours of the moment are drawn over SDO/AIA 171 Å, AIA 193 Å or HMI magnetogram images: a full-disk view on the left and a zoomed inset of the chosen observation.

  • Instrument toggles show or hide each instrument (oi= in the address); the image buttons switch channel (img=aia193, img=hmi).
  • The list gives every observation with its description and a link to its data. Choosing one, from the list or the image, centres the images on it (hl= in the address).
  • Solar Orbiter fields are carried into Earth's view through the solar surface; parts beyond Earth's limb are dashed.
  • Download PDF opens the print dialog with a report: the full disk and zoomed images with every footprint as vector outlines, then a table of each footprint with its instrument, identifier, start and end times, centre, description and data link, and a link back to the view. Choose Save as PDF as the destination. Download PNG appears for archived Cryo-NIRSP runs, whose images this site serves; other observations load their images live from Helioviewer, which a browser can print but this site cannot copy into a PNG.

Coordinated observations

"Find coordinated observations", under the observations panel, lists every time the chosen facilities (DKIST, IRIS, Hinode/EIS, Solar Orbiter) observed the same place on the Sun together: their observations overlap in time and their fields overlap in Earth's view. Narrow it by date and to the disk or the limb. Each result opens with all its footprints drawn. The search is kept in the address (find, ffrom, fto, fwhere), and Copy link to this search gives a link that opens it.

Sharing a view

The address of each page holds everything needed to reproduce it, and Copy link buttons on the map, the sky view, the observations panel and the finder copy it. Adding a section name after # opens the page at that section.

Data and updates

Positions come from JPL Horizons. Observation lists come from the DKIST Data Center, the LMSAL Heliophysics Coverage Registry (IRIS), the Hinode/EIS as-run catalogue and the Solar Orbiter Archive; SDO images from Helioviewer; synoptic magnetograms from JSOC; space-weather events from NASA DONKI. All of them are refreshed on the first of each month. How the numbers are computed and checked is on the methods page; problems and requests are welcome on GitHub.
